# Customer Avatar — Pro Lash Serum

> Two segments emerged in intake and research: **Extension Wearers** (the sharper, more urgent pain) and **Young Mothers** (the clean-ingredient buyer). Research supports treating "Extension Wearers" as the primary avatar — the founder's own origin story is built on this exact person — with "Young Mothers" as a secondary, overlapping segment. Where the two diverge, both are noted.

## Identity
- **Name (archetypal):** "Damaged-Lash Dana" — a woman who has worn lash extensions long enough that her natural lashes have visibly suffered for it.
- **Age range:** 22–38 (skews 25–34)
- **Gender:** Women (cis and trans)
- **Income / household income:** Mid-range disposable income for beauty spend — someone who already pays €50–100+/month on lash fills, so €19.99–35 for a serum is a minor add-on, not a stretch purchase.
- **Location / lifestyle context:** Urban/suburban, active on Instagram/TikTok, likely already a regular at a lash salon or was until the damage got bad enough to stop.
- **Occupation / day-to-day reality:** Wide range — office workers, service industry, new mothers on leave or back at work; unified less by job and more by the fact that lash extensions are part of a maintained "put-together" self-presentation routine.
- **Relationship and family status:** Mixed; the "Young Mothers" overlap segment is specifically postpartum or currently pregnant/nursing, newly ingredient-conscious in a way that spills over from skincare/nutrition into beauty.

## Psychographics
- **Core identity (how they see themselves):** Someone who takes care of her appearance as part of taking care of herself — not vain, *maintained*.
- **Identity they aspire to:** Low-maintenance-looking but high-maintenance-results — lashes that look done without needing a fill every 3 weeks or a mascara before leaving the house.
- **Identity they fear becoming:** Someone whose lashes are visibly thin/damaged/"used up" — a state she associates with letting herself go, or with the extensions having "ruined" something natural about her.
- **Values (top 3):** Ingredient honesty / "clean" living (especially the mother segment), visible results over vague promises, not being sold to with fear or hype.
- **Daily emotional state:** Low-grade self-consciousness about her lash line when bare-faced; mild guilt/regret about the extension habit; skepticism from having been burned by products that promised more than they delivered.

## The Problem
- **Surface-level pain (what they'd say out loud):** "My natural lashes are so thin/patchy from extensions, I can't go without falsies or a fill anymore."
- **Deeper pain (what they wouldn't admit at a dinner party):** She feels like she did this to herself — chasing a look and damaging the real thing underneath it — and worries it's not fully reversible.
- **Identity-level pain (what this problem says about who they are):** That she's dependent on an artificial fix to feel presentable; that her "natural" self isn't enough anymore.
- **How long they've had this problem:** Typically months to a few years of cumulative extension wear before the damage becomes undeniable.
- **What it has cost them:** Ongoing extension/fill spend (often €50–100+/month), the time cost of appointments, and — per the ~30% damage-incidence stat in research — real physical setback: breakage, thinning, or lash loss that then requires its own recovery time.

## Failed Solutions
- **What they've already tried:** Taking "lash breaks" (as technicians themselves recommend, 4–6 weeks), switching technicians, mascara to camouflage thinness, castor oil as a folk remedy.
- **Why it didn't work (in their words):** Breaks mean going out feeling under-done the whole time; castor oil is oily and risks loosening any extensions she does still wear; mascara treats the symptom, not the lash itself.
- **Who they blame:** Usually the extensions/lash techs, or resign to "this is just how my lashes are now."
- **What they secretly fear is true about themselves:** That she waited too long, or did too many cycles of extensions, and the damage is now closer to permanent than temporary.

## Desired Outcome
- **Surface-level want:** Longer, thicker, stronger natural lashes — visibly, within weeks.
- **Deeper want:** The *option* to go without extensions or falsies and still feel done — freedom from the maintenance cycle, not necessarily abandoning it entirely.
- **Identity-level transformation:** From "my lashes are ruined" to "my lashes are strong" — reclaiming a small but emotionally loaded piece of physical resilience.
- **How life looks 6 months after the problem is solved:** She can skip a fill appointment without anxiety, apply the serum as a 10-second nightly habit, and — for the mother segment specifically — feel like she's back to taking care of herself in a small, visible way post-pregnancy.

## Beliefs (Currently)
- **What they currently believe about the category:** "Lash serums work, but a lot of them irritate your eyes or aren't something you want to use long-term" (directly reinforced by real competitor complaint volume — see `deepresearch.md` Section 3).
- **What they currently believe about solutions like ours:** Skeptical by default — "I've tried serums before and nothing happened" is the near-universal opening stance in sourced reviews before conversion.
- **What they currently believe about themselves:** That the damage might not be fully her fault to fix — a belief the founder's own product (formulated by someone who watched this exact problem for six years) can directly speak to.

## Objections
**Top 5, ranked by likelihood:**
1. "Will this irritate my eyes the way [competitor] did for other people?"
2. "Can I actually use this while I still have extensions on?"
3. "Is 'organic' just a marketing word, or does it work as fast as the harsher stuff?"
4. "Why is this cheaper than Nanolash/UKLash — is it weaker?"
5. "I've tried lash serums before and saw nothing."

**Single objection most likely to kill the sale:** #1 — the eye-irritation/safety fear, because it's the one most directly reinforced by real, documented complaints against named competitors.

**Words they distrust / find salesy:** miracle (used ironically in reviews, not brand-safe as a claim), guaranteed overnight results, clinically proven (without a citable study), "as seen on."

## Where They Are
- **Social platforms they live on:** Instagram, TikTok (both categories saturated with lash-serum before/after content and comparison reviews).
- **Authorities / creators they trust:** Lash technicians and beauty creators doing multi-month honest-review formats (the "used it for 3+ months" review structure recurs across sourced content) — not brand-paid influencers making day-one claims.
- **Communities they belong to:** Beauty/skincare forums and comment sections (Reddit-adjacent, TikTok comments), lash-extension client communities via their salons.
- **What they're already buying in adjacent categories:** Extension fills/lash services, mascara, and — for the mother segment — pregnancy-safe skincare (a market independently valued at $3.2B in 2025, per research), meaning they're already primed to pay for "clean" formulations elsewhere in their routine.
